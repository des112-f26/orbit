Orbit

An old copper engraving set against a dark, starry night sky. The yellow drum sun sits in the middle, pulsing its rays and always turning its longest ray toward the Earth. Twelve small circles ring the sun, strung together with drum ornaments that pulse in time with the sun; each holds a story of its own, told in black-and-white drum figures: first the assimilation of the Sámi (a drum taken and burned, a church rising as the goahti fades, a child taken to the boarding school and leaving the four winds hat behind, the joik crossed out), then a rich Norwegian girl and a Sámi boy whose love is forbidden (they meet, fall in love, are torn apart by her father and the church, and meet again in secret), and last their child, born into a komse, caught between the house and the goahti, turned away by Norwegian and Sámi children alike, and left a stranger in both worlds. The Earth is the big circle: it moves slowly from small circle to small circle and plays each story large as it passes, for as long as the long hand of a clock takes to go round once, while a frame of drum ornaments fills in round it one by one just behind the Moon, so the ring is complete when the story is over. The Moon circles the Earth once per story, lit on the side facing the sun; hold and drag the Earth or the Moon, or turn the sun like a dial, to move between and through the stories, and northern lights pour out of the Earth while you hold it
